Good floor liners are one of the best-value upgrades you can make to a car. They keep snow, salt, mud and spilled coffee
off the carpet, protect resale value, and they're easier to clean than any factory mat. The catch is fit: a liner that's
even slightly wrong can leave gaps for water to get through, curl at the edges, or slide toward the pedals.

This guide ranks six options, from premium laser-measured liners to a budget universal set, scores each out of 10, and
explains how to get the right fit for your exact vehicle the first time.

## The best floor mats and liners, ranked

<Pick id="tuxmat" products={frontmatter.products} rank={1}>

TuxMat takes a different approach from the rubber-tray brands. Its mats use a layered construction with a waterproof vinyl
surface, and they're cut to cover the whole footwell and run up the sidewalls. Car and Driver tested them in a BMW i4 and
named them Best Overall, saying they offered the most comprehensive coverage of any mats in the test. The second-row mats
were so thorough the testers had to lift the rear seat cushion to tuck them in.

They also don't squeak underfoot, a common complaint with hard liners. They cost more than most rivals, and TuxMat's
vehicle list is shorter than WeatherTech's or Husky's, so check that your car is covered.

</Pick>

<Pick id="weathertech-floorliner" products={frontmatter.products} rank={2}>

WeatherTech is the name most people know, and for good reason. Its FloorLiners are laser-measured, designed and made in
the USA, and available for a huge range of vehicles, including many new models. Car and Driver installed them in a Hyundai
Ioniq 5 and described the fit, once anchored, as perfect.

There are two lines. The standard FloorLiner uses a rigid High-Density Tri-Extruded (HDTE) material, which holds its tray
shape but is very firm and can be a wrestle to install. The FloorLiner HP is softer and more flexible; C&D found it easier
to install and remove, and it cleaned up better. One independent comparison found the standard WeatherTech slid about an
inch on wet pavement in an SUV with smooth carpet, so make sure the retention hooks are engaged.

</Pick>

<Pick id="husky-weatherbeater" products={frontmatter.products} rank={3}>

Husky's WeatherBeater liners are the best value in custom fit. They're laser-measured, made in the USA, and covered by a
limited lifetime guarantee for the original owner and vehicle. Car and Driver tested them in a Honda Accord and said they
installed in about five minutes and settled perfectly into the footwell contours.

Hundreds of small "StayPut Nibs" on the underside grip the carpet, and in an independent side-by-side test, the Husky liner
didn't move during hard braking on wet pavement. Husky's lip is also a little taller than WeatherTech's, which helps with
gravel and snow off work boots. The trade-off is that the TPO material is firmer and can stiffen in deep cold.

</Pick>

<Pick id="3d-maxpider-kagu" products={frontmatter.products} rank={4}>

3D MAXpider's Kagu liners are the comfortable choice. They use three layers: a rubbery TPR top, a cushioned XPE foam core,
and an anti-skid backing. That makes them softer and quieter underfoot than hard trays, which many EV and car owners prefer.

They're popular with Tesla owners, but fit reports are mixed: some Model Y owners say they fit perfectly, while others
report gaps where water and dirt reach the carpet. The warranty is three years, shorter than the lifetime coverage from
WeatherTech, Husky and TuxMat.

</Pick>

<Pick id="smartliner" products={frontmatter.products} rank={5}>

SMARTLINER is Car and Driver's Best Budget custom-fit pick. Its liners are made from a recyclable plastic-rubber and
polyethylene blend, and C&D found them the most tub-like of all the mats it tested, which is exactly what you want in
snow country. Installation in C&D's long-term Rivian R1T was simple.

The rigid material that makes them hold water can make them hard to squeeze into smaller footwells, and they look more
utilitarian than premium liners. For trucks and SUVs, that's an easy trade for the price.

</Pick>

<Pick id="motor-trend-flextough" products={frontmatter.products} rank={6}>

If you drive an older or less common vehicle, or you just need protection on a budget, Motor Trend's FlexTough mats are
a sensible stopgap. The set includes two front mats (27 × 20 inches) and a rear runner (51 × 14 inches) in heavy-duty
rubber, with trim lines so you can cut them closer to your footwell.

Universal mats will never match a custom liner's coverage, and they don't lock into the factory anchors. After trimming,
check that the driver's mat can't slide forward toward the pedals.

</Pick>

## How to get the right fit

Most fit problems come from ordering for the wrong version of the right car. Before you buy, write down:

1. **Year, make and model**, plus **body style** (sedan, hatchback, crew cab, extended cab).
2. **Trim and drivetrain.** AWD versions, hybrids and EVs often have different floor pans.
3. **Seating layout.** Second-row bench or captain's chairs, and whether there's a third row.
4. **Floor details.** Factory retention hooks or posts, a center hump, a console that slides, or under-floor storage.
5. **Which rows you need.** Listings may cover the first row only, first and second rows, or a full set including cargo.

Then use the brand's vehicle selector or Amazon's fit check, and read the listing notes. If your vehicle had a mid-year
change, confirm the build date.

## Custom liners vs. universal mats

| | Custom-fit liners | Universal mats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage | Full footwell, raised walls | Flat area only, gaps at edges |
| Liquid containment | High | Low to moderate |
| Stays in place | Uses factory anchors | Relies on grip; must be checked |
| Price | Higher | Low |
| Moves to your next car | Rarely | Usually |

## Safety: keep mats away from the pedals

NHTSA warns that loose or incompatible floor mats can interfere with the accelerator or brake. Use only one mat per
footwell, never stack a new mat on top of an old one, and always fasten the driver's mat to the factory retention hooks.
After installation, press the accelerator and brake all the way to make sure nothing catches.

## Material guide

- **Rigid TPE or HDTE (WeatherTech FloorLiner):** holds its shape and contains liquid well; can be firm and slippery when
  wet.
- **TPO (Husky WeatherBeater):** durable with good grip; can stiffen in extreme cold.
- **Foam-core TPR (3D MAXpider Kagu):** cushioned and quiet; edges are lower.
- **Vinyl-surfaced layers (TuxMat):** maximum coverage and a carpet-like look.
- **Rubber (Motor Trend FlexTough):** cheap and flexible; universal fit only.
