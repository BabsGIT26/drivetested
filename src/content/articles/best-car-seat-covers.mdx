Seat covers do two jobs: they protect upholstery from dirt, sweat, spills, pets and work clothes, and they can refresh
worn or stained seats for a lot less than reupholstering. The right cover depends on what you're protecting against. A
contractor's truck, a family SUV with a dog and a commuter car in Phoenix all need something different.

This guide ranks six covers for different jobs, scores each one out of 10, and explains the safety detail that matters
most: side airbags.

## How to choose seat covers

### Custom vs. universal

| | Custom-fit | Universal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fit | Cut for your exact seats | Stretch-to-fit; can slide |
| Access to controls, armrests, belts | Openings in the right places | Often blocked or awkward |
| Side airbags | Patterned for your seat | Look for an airbag-safe rating |
| Price | High | Low |

### Match the material to the job

- **Duck weave or canvas:** toughest for work and outdoor use.
- **Neoprene-style (NeoSupreme):** water resistant and soft; can feel warm.
- **Breathable linen or mesh:** coolest in summer; less spill-proof.
- **Faux leather:** wipe-clean and leather-look; hot in the sun.
- **Waterproof hammock or protector:** best for dogs and messy cargo.

### Check side airbags first

Many front seats have a side airbag in the outer bolster, marked with a small "airbag" tag. A cover that isn't designed
for it can delay or block deployment. Choose covers rated for seats with side airbags and install them exactly as
directed, with the airbag seam facing the door.

### Think about heated and ventilated seats

Thick or waterproof covers can reduce heating and block ventilation. If you use those features a lot, choose a thinner,
breathable cover and check the listing.

### Installation tips

- Remove headrests first, and tuck straps through the seat crease before hooking them underneath.
- Keep seat-belt buckles, LATCH anchors and seat controls clear.
- Re-tension straps after a few days of use, once the fabric settles.
