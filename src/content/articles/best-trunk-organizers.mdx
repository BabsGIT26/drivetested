A trunk organizer stops grocery bags from tipping over on the first corner, keeps your emergency gear in one place, and
makes it easy to find the jumper pack in the dark. It's a small purchase that makes a car feel noticeably calmer.

This guide ranks five organizers, from a rugged bin that will outlast the car to a cheap collapsible crate, scores each
one out of 10, and covers how to size one for your trunk and keep it from sliding.

  These picks are research-based: manufacturer sizes and specs, warranty terms, owner-review patterns and published

## The best trunk organizers, ranked

<Pick id="thule-go-box-large" products={frontmatter.products} rank={1}>

Thule's Go Box is built like the rest of the brand's gear: tough, water-resistant and simple. It folds flat when you need
the whole trunk, has a built-in carry handle, and a fold-over cap keeps rain and prying eyes off your stuff.

Car and Driver named it Best Overall. Its testers praised the side pockets, which use strong elastic bands with tensioners
so you can cinch them around whatever you're carrying, and the high-density plastic fasteners that hold it together when
collapsed. It handled a full grocery load and aggressive driving well. The drawbacks are weight (it's heavy even when
empty) and price. This Large version is 24 × 18 × 12 inches and 80 liters; Thule also makes a Medium.

</Pick>

<Pick id="fortem-trunk-organizer" products={frontmatter.products} rank={2}>

FORTEM's organizer is one of the most popular on Amazon, and it gets the important things right for a reasonable price.
Rigid base plates support every side panel, so it doesn't collapse when half empty. Non-slip strips on the bottom and
adjustable straps with metal hooks keep it in place, and a removable lid hides what's inside.

This standard model is 22 × 14.5 × 12.5 inches with up to three compartments and 65 liters of space. You can collapse it to
a single compartment for the back seat, or link several together. FORTEM also sells larger six-compartment versions.

</Pick>

<Pick id="drive-trunk-organizer" products={frontmatter.products} rank={3}>

DRIVE's organizer is Car and Driver's Best Budget pick. It shares the basic layout of pricier organizers, with multiple
compartments, side pockets and a collapsible design, plus a strap system to anchor it.

The difference is in materials. C&D found it tends to flop and lose shape after repeated use, and the elastic-loop closures
didn't inspire confidence. For light loads and occasional use, it's fine for the money.

</Pick>

<Pick id="greenmade-instacrate" products={frontmatter.products} rank={4}>

Sometimes a crate is all you need. GreenMade's InstaCrate is a rigid plastic bin that folds flat in seconds and stacks,
which makes it ideal for grocery runs, sports equipment, or moving things between the car and the house. GreenMade says
it's made in the USA.

Car and Driver liked its versatility, though it has just one compartment and no pockets, lid or straps. Put it on a
rubber cargo mat so it doesn't slide.

</Pick>

<Pick id="tidify-front-seat-organizer" products={frontmatter.products} rank={5}>

Not every car needs trunk storage. If you work from your car, Tidify's front-seat organizer hangs from the headrest and
puts a padded laptop sleeve, deep pockets, bottle holders and smaller pockets within arm's reach. Side straps keep it
stable.

Car and Driver liked the number of pockets and easy driver access, but a strap buckle broke during installation, and it

</Pick>

## How to choose a trunk organizer

### Measure first

Measure the width and depth of your cargo floor and the height under the cargo cover or parcel shelf. Leave room to lift
items in and out. Sedans usually suit a medium organizer; SUVs, wagons and minivans can take a large one or two side by side.

### Rigid, semi-rigid or collapsible?

| Type | Pros | Cons | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rugged fabric bin | Durable, water-resistant, folds | Heavier, pricier | Thule Go Box |
| Fabric with stiff panels | Light, cheap, many pockets | Can sag over time | FORTEM, DRIVE |
| Plastic crate | Rigid, stackable, easy to clean | One compartment, no lid | InstaCrate |

### Anchoring

Sliding is the most common complaint. Look for straps or hooks that attach to the cargo tie-downs, non-slip strips
underneath, or both. A rubber cargo liner helps any organizer stay put.

### Lids and pockets

A lid hides valuables and keeps things from flying around in a hard stop. Side pockets and mesh panels keep small items
such as cables, wipes and tire gauges findable.
