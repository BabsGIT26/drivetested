faq:
  - q: How many peak amps do I need?
    a: >-
      Don't shop on peak amps. There's no common standard for measuring them, so the numbers aren't comparable. Use the
      manufacturer's engine rating instead and choose one comfortably above your engine size, with more headroom for
      diesels, cold climates and older vehicles.
  - q: Can I leave a lithium jump starter in the car?
    a: >-
      Most are designed for it, but check the storage temperature range in the manual. Extreme heat and deep cold shorten
      battery life, and many lithium packs won't charge below freezing. Recharge after every use and every few months.
  - q: Will a jump starter fix a bad battery?
    a: >-
      No. It gets you started, but if the battery keeps going flat it may be worn out, or the alternator or a parasitic
      drain may be the problem. Have the battery and charging system tested.
  - q: Can I use a jump starter on a hybrid or EV?
    a: >-
      Hybrids and EVs have a 12-volt battery that can go flat, but procedures vary by model. Follow the owner's manual,
      use only the 12-volt jump points it describes, and never connect to the high-voltage system.
  - q: Is it safe to use boost or override mode?
    a: >-
      Boost or manual override sends power to a battery too dead for the unit to detect. It also disables some safety
      checks, so confirm polarity carefully and follow the manual before you press it.

A dead battery is the most common reason a car won't start, and it always happens at the worst time. A portable jump
starter lets you fix it yourself in a couple of minutes, without waiting for roadside assistance or flagging down another
driver with cables. Modern lithium units are small enough for a glovebox and hold their charge for months.

This is our main jump starter guide. It ranks six models for different vehicles and budgets, scores each one out of 10,
and explains how to match one to your engine. If you're new to jump starters, our step-by-step
[how to choose a jump starter](/jump-starters/how-to-choose/) guide covers the basics in more depth. Want a tire

## How to choose a jump starter

### Start with your engine, not peak amps

Peak amps are the least useful number on the box because there's no standard way to measure them. Use the manufacturer's
**engine rating** (for example "8.0L gas / 6.0L diesel") and choose a model rated above your engine. Give yourself
extra headroom for:

- **Diesel engines**, which need more power to crank
- **Cold climates**, where batteries lose capacity and oil thickens
- **Older vehicles** and weak starters that need longer cranking

### Battery capacity (Wh) decides how many jumps you get

Watt-hours (Wh) tell you how much energy is stored. More Wh means more jumps between charges and more phone charging on a
trip. If a unit lists only milliamp-hours (mAh), divide by about 270 for a rough Wh figure at typical lithium cell voltage
(mAh × 3.7 ÷ 1,000). Independent tests often find usable capacity below the claim; TechGearLab measured the GB40 at
about 65% of its rating.

### Lithium vs. lead-acid

- **Lithium** (most picks here): compact, light and holds a charge for months. Sensitive to extreme heat and cold.
- **Lead-acid** (Clore JNC770R): heavy and bulky but rugged, with lots of stored energy. Needs regular charging.

### Clamps and cables

Independent testers complain about short cables and stubby clamps more than anything else. If your battery sits under a
cover or against the firewall, longer cables and slim, well-shaped clamps matter more than an extra 1,000 peak amps.

### Safety features

Look for reverse-polarity and spark protection, and understand how the unit's boost or override mode works. Override
modes bypass some protections so they can start a battery that reads nearly zero volts.

### Charging and extras

USB-C Power Delivery means faster recharging from a common charger. Check whether a wall charger is included. Many units
double as power banks and flashlights, and some (like the HULKMAN) add a 12V output for accessories.

## How to jump-start a car with a portable jump starter

1. **Check your owner's manual** for jump points and any special steps. Don't jump a cracked, leaking or frozen battery.
2. **Turn the ignition and accessories off.**
3. **Connect the red clamp to positive (+)**, then the black clamp to negative or an unpainted metal ground point.
4. **Switch the jump starter on** and wait for the ready light.
5. **Start the car.** Don't crank longer than the manual allows; rest between tries.
6. **Disconnect black first, then red**, and recharge the jump starter.
