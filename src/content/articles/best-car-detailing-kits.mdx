faq:
  - q: Is a foam cannon worth it?
    a: >-
      A pressure-washer foam cannon loosens dirt before you touch the paint, which reduces swirl marks, and it makes
      washing faster. Garden-hose foam guns help too, but the foam is thinner. If you don't own a pressure washer, a good
      two-bucket wash with a grit guard is still very safe for paint.
  - q: What's the two-bucket method?
    a: >-
      One bucket holds soapy water and the other holds plain rinse water. After each panel, rinse the mitt in the clean
      bucket (ideally over a grit guard) before reloading with soap, so you don't drag dirt back onto the paint.
  - q: How often should I wax my car?
    a: >-
      Follow the product's directions, but a traditional carnauba wax usually lasts weeks to a couple of months. Water
      beading less is a good sign it's time to reapply. Sealants and ceramic sprays generally last longer.
  - q: Can I use dish soap to wash my car?
    a: >-
      It's best not to. Dish soap is designed to strip grease and can remove wax and sealant. A dedicated car shampoo
      cleans without stripping protection.
  - q: What does a clay bar do?
    a: >-
      Clay removes bonded contamination (the gritty feel on clean paint) before you polish or wax. Use plenty of lubricant,
      work small areas and fold the clay often. Most cars only need it once or twice a year.

Washing your own car is cheaper than a detailer, gentler on paint than most automatic washes, and oddly satisfying. A
good kit gets you started with the right chemicals and tools in one box, and makes a great gift. The trouble is that
"kits" range from a few bottles to a bucket stuffed with foam guns, towels and brushes, and the piece counts on the box
can be misleading.

This guide ranks six kits for different needs and budgets, scores each one out of 10, and explains what a good kit should
include.

<Callout title="How we chose and scored these">
  These picks are research-based: manufacturer descriptions of what's in each kit, owner-review patterns and published
  independent evaluations (mainly Car and Driver's comparison of kits). We haven't finished our own hands-on testing
  yet, so the score out of 10 is a research score, not a hands-on rating. [Learn how we test and score](/how-we-test/).
</Callout>

## The best car detailing kits, ranked

<Pick id="chemical-guys-16-piece" products={frontmatter.products} rank={1}>

Chemical Guys' Arsenal Builder is a perennial Amazon best seller, and the 16-piece version is the most complete wash kit
for anyone with a pressure washer. It includes a foam cannon, a heavy-duty bucket with grit guard, six 16-ounce bottles of
shampoo, wheel cleaner and protectants, plus a microfiber wash mitt, drying towels, a detailing brush and applicators.

Car and Driver named it the Best Premium kit, praising the substantial bottles, sturdy spray nozzles and the fact that
the whole kit fits inside the bucket. The foam cannon is the key piece: thick pre-wash foam lifts dirt before your mitt
touches the paint. It's pricey, and it's only worth it if you have a pressure washer.

</Pick>

<Pick id="adams-essential-wash-kit" products={frontmatter.products} rank={2}>

Adam's kit is the one to get if you don't own a pressure washer. Its foam sprayer runs off a garden hose, and Car and
Driver named the kit Best All-in-One, saying the tool and towel collection was the best of the bunch and the foam sprayer
was well made.

It comes with a large bucket and grit guard and 16-ounce sprays of nearly every cleaner you'd want. One caveat: the
"24-piece" count includes three air fresheners. It's priced close to the premium Chemical Guys kit, but it's an excellent
gift for someone starting out.

</Pick>

<Pick id="meguiars-complete-car-care" products={frontmatter.products} rank={3}>

Most wash kits help you clean and protect. Meguiar's Complete Car Care Kit also helps you *fix*. Alongside Gold Class car
wash, a carnauba-plus liquid wax and tire gel, it includes clay bars with Quik Detailer to remove bonded contamination,
ScratchX for light scratches and swirls, and PlastX for cloudy plastic like headlight lenses. There's an interior detailer
too.

It doesn't include a bucket, foam tool or brushes, so you'll need your own. Paint correction takes patience; start with
a small test area.

</Pick>

<Pick id="chemical-guys-14-piece" products={frontmatter.products} rank={4}>

The 14-piece Arsenal Builder (HOL126) is the garden-hose version of Chemical Guys' kit. Its TORQ Foam Blaster 6 attaches
to a standard hose, and the kit includes Honeydew Snow Foam, Diablo wheel cleaner, Silk Shine dressing, Butter Wet Wax
and glass cleaner, plus a bucket with grit guard, mitt, towels and applicators.

Garden-hose foam is thinner than what a foam cannon produces, and a detailing blog review noted durability complaints
about the foam gun. It's still a complete starter kit that costs less than the 16-piece version.

</Pick>

<Pick id="griots-garage-starter" products={frontmatter.products} rank={5}>

Griot's Garage skips the tools and focuses on chemicals. The kit includes car wash, wheel cleaner, synthetic clay, Speed
Shine quick detailer, Best of Show wax and vinyl and rubber dressing. Car and Driver named it the Best Wash and Wax Kit,
calling its sprayers the nicest of any kit it used, and noted the soap is concentrated enough to need only half an ounce
per gallon.

The only accessory is a wax applicator sponge, so you'll need your own bucket, mitt and towels. If you already have those,
it's a quality upgrade.

</Pick>

<Pick id="meguiars-classic-wash-wax" products={frontmatter.products} rank={6}>

Meguiar's Classic Wash & Wax Kit is a simple, affordable way to start. It includes Gold Class car wash, a one-step cleaner
wax, Hot Rims wheel and tire cleaner, Quik Detailer and an interior detailer, plus microfiber and sponges.

Car and Driver called it an Excellent Value pick, praising the large bottles and quality microfiber. You'll need your own
bucket, and there's no tire brush.

</Pick>

## How to choose

### Do you have a pressure washer?

If yes, a foam cannon kit (Chemical Guys 16-piece) gives the best pre-wash. If not, choose a garden-hose foam gun kit
(Adam's or Chemical Guys 14-piece), or a traditional bucket kit.

### Wash, protect, or correct?

- **Wash and protect:** any of the kits here.
- **Correct light scratches and contamination:** Meguiar's Complete, with clay and ScratchX.

### Tools vs. chemicals

All-in-one kits are best for beginners and gifts. If you already own buckets, mitts and towels, a chemicals-only kit like
Griot's gives you better products for the money.

### Don't trust the piece count

Bottles, caps, buckets, lids and even air fresheners can count as "pieces." Read the actual contents list.

## A simple wash routine

1. **Work in the shade** on cool paint.
2. **Clean wheels and tires first** with a dedicated cleaner and brush.
3. **Pre-rinse** (or foam) to remove loose dirt.
4. **Two-bucket wash** from the top down, rinsing the mitt often.
5. **Rinse and dry** with a clean microfiber drying towel.
6. **Protect** with wax or a spray sealant, then dress tires and clean glass.
